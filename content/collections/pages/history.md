    heading: "The 1977 Silver Jubilee"
    content: |-
      The annual Scalby Fair grew out of the Silver Jubilee celebrations for Her Majesty the Queen in 1977. The Jubilee celebrations took the form of a giant street party with tables down the centre of the High Street from the Nags Head to St Laurence’s Church. The idea came from a meeting in the Nags Head involving Ken Middleton, Mike Hodges, Jimmy Gray, Stewart Woodhead, Mick Lawson and Geoff Dove. Ken became Chairman, Mike Hodges the Secretary and Jimmy Gray the Treasurer and they invited Tom Laughton CBE to be the President. Marie Gill did a lot of the fundraising. The idea for the Silver Jubilee was that we should organise a few teas for the old folks of the parish/village. It snowballed into 1600 (sixteen hundred teas and sixteen hundred jubilee crowns were also given out) the teas were made in a conveyor belt system in the Scalby Parish Hall which included made up plates on which were sandwiches and cakes and fruit which were sealed and these plated teas were transported to Methodist Hall (by Woodhead vans) and were distributed where the people sat at tables in the street. This was all funded by door to door collections, raffles in the local pubs, coffee mornings, a Three Peaks Walk and a jumble sale in Parish Hall. The first event was in early summer 1977 when a pageant and musical evening was held at Scalby Parish Hall. Who will ever forget Joe Greenan and John Smith in their lederhosen singing about their bread basket and their tinkle hausen? Or Cliff Poskett who must have been well into his 80’s painting all those magnificent shields (some of which are still in the Nags Head store) and performing on a hobby horse “Here Comes the Galloping Major”.

      There was great enthusiasm and it was a highly successful event blessed by dry weather. The funds raised paid for the floodlighting of St Laurences Church and renovation of the fountain (although not its water supply).

    heading: "The birth of Scalby Fair"
    content: |-
      Following the success of the Silver Jubilee it was then that the Scalby Fair Committee was formed in early 1978 at the AGM of the Silver Jubilee Committee. Scalby Fair Committee arose with the intention of keeping the community spirit alive. The rest is history.

      A meeting was called in the Church Rooms out of which a Committee was formed in early 1977 and regular meetings were held on Sunday mornings in the Nags Head at which Tom Laughton said how impressed he was at the way that the tasks were divided and everyone played an active part in planning the weekend.

      On the day, many people dressed in red, white and blue and there was a huge entry for the children’s fancy dress. There was a celebration dinner/dance for committee members, wives, friends and helpers held at the Raven Hall Hotel, paid for by themselves and organised by Carol Grayson who was the secretary at the time.

      On the Sunday, Mick Lawson organised a Jubilee Walk over twelve miles through Harwood Dale starting and finishing at the Nags Head. We had tremendous help from the North Yorkshire Moors Search and Rescue Team and the Lions Club.

      The weekend was so successful that the idea of Scalby Fair was born. The planning started straight away, the fund raising and thoughts on a week of events.

    content: |-
      Tom Laughton stayed on as President until his death in 1984, Ken Middleton continued as President until Dave Richardson took over in 1983, Dave was a very hard working Secretary in 1978 and 1979 and I took over as Treasurer for a year until I realised how much easier it was to have an accountant doing the job and handed over to Brian Connolly. When I took over as Secretary in 1981 Dave did two years as Treasurer before Alex Johnson took over for a long stint in the post and we have had some good secretaries through the years, including Sheila Crapper, Dave Richmond, Cathy Wood and Arnold Wilson.

    heading: "Stalls, performances and community events"
    content: |-
      But, let us remember where all the hard work was done. The inspiration for the stalls, erecting them, dismantling them and storing them as well as renewing and replacing where necessary was down to Geoff Dove and his early helpers included Alan Bowker, Pete Colley, retired Superintendent of Police Jim Ward and Nev Flinton. Marie Gill was very much the catalyst for the fund raising and pushed us all hard to make sure we had sufficient funds each year for the following year’s Fair. Dave Richardson created a commemorative Scalby Fair programme, a wonderful publication full of interesting information about the Village and its organisations as well as publicising the events of Scalby Fair for the coming year. Dave, and many others, used to go round as many houses as he could selling the programmes door to door and encouraged others to help him.

      We started with stalls on the High Street and it was a few years later when we extended into South Street. Recalling many of the events of the Scalby Fair week brings back many happy memories. The Donkeys up the drive way to Goldenfields, the Morris Dancers and the Irish Dancers, the Punch and Judy all on the Fair Day together with Scalby Fair photographic competition and the annual children’s fancy dress. Anne Tubbs provided cream teas in the Vicarage Garden and Mahlo Hollywood kept the helpers well watered with cups of tea.

    heading: "The first Flower Festivals"
    content: |-
      And then, there was the Flower Festival. Those wonderful ladies Mrs Wharton and Mrs Beal who gathered the arrangers round them and produced fantastic displays in both St Laurence’s Church and the Methodist Church. They were followed by Rosemary Grace, Jan Milnes and Anne Tubbs. In the early years, Ken Gill would take orders for flowers and go to Leeds Market to collect. Reading a list of contributors shows how many are still contributing to the Flower Festival but so many others are no longer with us.

    heading: "Walks, runs and sporting events"
    content: |-
      In the first year, Alan Grundy produced a video of Scalby Fair. In those days, video equipment was bulky and it was remarkable to see it set above the High Street and how he got the equipment to the top of the Church Tower goodness knows!

      We have had some tremendous stalwarts over the years with great help from the Lions Club and the Rescue Team. Carol Grayson was Minute Secretary for some years and organised the Scalby Fair Dance. Mick Lawson continued to organise a walk and the Twelve Mile Three Ridges Walk was a fixture for some years. It took us over Raincliffe Woods, Scalby Nab and Scalby Beacon round the back of Hackness Hall. Something worth doing again I think?

      The Nab Run starting from the Rosette Inn used to go up Scalby Nab and was attended by hundreds to begin with. We had great help from Scarborough Athletic Club. I managed to keep hold of the starting gun each year so I was never able to take part! Mary had happy memories of driving the car with flashing lights in front of the runners. The Rescue Team kept everyone in radio contact and Ken Middleton and Ann Beavis inscribed certificates for all who took part.

      The junior disco was very successful until the schools realised the potential and school discos started in the area. The first year, we had Dave Beck the barefooted DJ to entertain. Although the Scalby Fair Queen was chosen from 10, 11 and 12 year olds, first at the Nab Run but later at the junior disco. It was with great pride that Mrs Wharton made the Queen’s dress and she was crowned each year at Scalby Fair.

    heading: "Fair Week traditions"
    content: |-
      The open garden day was started by Tom Laughton in his garden at Southbeck House but after his death it moved to the Miner’s Home, Low Hall where Nev Flinton was the groundsman.

      The Treasure Hunt on foot through the village is still popular today with the expectation that the winner sets the next Hunt.

      Ken and Marie Gill also organised hanging baskets along the street and we had that wonderful old barrel on wheels and pump, with a rota set up to water the flowers each day.

      The Sports Day in the early years was organised by Mick Holder and Jenny Legg and there was a great opportunity for a large picnic on Scalby Cricket and Football Field.

      On the Sunday evening of the Fair there was a service of Thanksgiving which was instigated by the Reverend Christopher Tubbs who himself was always very active in the activities of the Fair.

    heading: "Pantomimes and the social heart of the Fair"
    content: |-
      The Nags Head was always the centre of the week and Ken Middleton was the landlord for some years. Not only was he the Chairman of the Fair but he and Mick Lawson could also be relied upon for any art work required.

      There were plays in the Parish Hall performed by Outsiders Theatre Group, there were gym displays at the Open Garden Evening, there were cricket matches (I remember my hamstrings going as the Nags Head beat the Plough) and in the mid-1980’s Scalby Fair put on its first pantomime, Scalberella directed by Gordon Truefitt, produced by my wife Mary, Musical Director Jack Jowcey, the three of them put on the first three annual pantomimes with a real cross section of the village taking part.

    heading: "Flower festivals and church events"
    content: |-
      Scalby Flower Festival originated at the time of the Queen’s Silver Jubilee when the Scalby Fair Committee thought that it would be another event to enhance the week’s programme, to involve more people from the village and to perhaps interest people from outside the village.

      Welcome Aboard! (3) by Jenny Johnson & Jenny Winn
      At the beginning both St Laurence’s Church and the Methodist Church were asked to be involved and for many years the two churches alternated as the venue. More recently, the Flower Festival has taken place each year at St Laurence’s over Scalby Fair weekend with members of both congregations taking part in this themed event linked with Scalby Fair. Arrangers come not only from the churches, but also from the local community including support from a number of members of Scarborough Flower Club, so the resulting displays are varied and imaginative.

      Over the last few years we have had “Flower Power” based on the events of the 1960s, “By The Seaside” with buckets and spades, donkey rides, lifeboats etc. and last year “Life on the Ocean Waves – A World Cruise”. When the Flower Festival first began, themes were more religiously based with topics such as “Psalms”, “Hymns”, “Church Festivals” etc. However, no matter how testing the theme may be, it is amazing how the arrangers always come up with something new and exciting. Although we are always pleased to welcome new volunteers, quite a number of people have been arranging for most of the last 35 years! That must warrant some applause!

      The Friday night preview of flowers has taken place over many years and, as with the topics, its format has also varied, from “sit and listen” entertainment, followed by a finger buffet, to the present, perhaps lower key and less financially demanding, viewing of flowers accompanied by Jack Jowsey playing the piano. Recent events have been happy and relaxed so this format will continue in 2019.

      Sea Shanties (2) by June Flood
      The Preview on the Friday (the day before Fair Day) is from 7.30pm to 9pm, with the previous informal walk round with music playing,

      On Fair Day Saturday itself, the church opens from 10am to 5pm for viewing with donations towards the Fair’s charities,

      On the day after, the Sunday, the church is open from approximately 10am to 5pm for viewing with donations towards the Fair’s charities. The opening time is provisional as the Sunday Services have to be confirmed each year by the vicar.

      On the Monday, the church is open from 10am to 3pm for viewing with donations towards the Fair’s charities.

      We hope that visitors viewing the displays at times other than the preview evening will be prepared to make a donation towards the flowers, which will help the Scalby Fair Committee’s donations to local causes.
